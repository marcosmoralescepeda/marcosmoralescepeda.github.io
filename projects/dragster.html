Wooden Dragster

1st place out of 28 cars, 0.972 seconds

A CO2-powered dragster my team took from four concepts to a carved wooden car that won the class race. I later ran an airflow study to see exactly where it makes drag.

The race

Every team raced its car down the same CO2 track and was ranked by time. Ours was the only car under one second.

Step 1: Four concepts

Each team member modeled their own design in SolidWorks. We compared them on drag, weight, stability, and how easy they would be to cut from wood.

Step 2: The final design

We picked a shape that cut drag while staying strong and fitting the CO2 cartridge rules: a tapered nose, smooth splines along the body, and a solid rear block to hold the cartridge.

Step 3: Cutting it from wood

We traced the profile onto a wood block, cut it on the band saw, then sanded and shaped it by hand until it matched the CAD model.

Step 4: The finished car

Painted and fitted with wheels and axles. The finished car keeps every feature of the CAD model: the pointed nose, the slim body, and the raised rear block that holds the CO2 cartridge.

Step 5: Airflow study

After the race I ran an external flow study in SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation to see why the shape worked and where it could improve. The car sits inside a virtual wind tunnel. The air comes straight at the nose, like the car is racing forward.

Why 20 m/s

That is a typical top speed for a small CO2 dragster, so the results reflect what the car actually sees on the track.

What the air does

Behind the rear

Does it make sense?

A quick hand check

When moving air hits a surface head-on, its pressure rises by about

½ ρ v² = ½ × 1.2 kg/m³ × (20 m/s)² ≈ 240 Pa

The simulation shows a pressure spread of about 290 Pa around the car, the same order of magnitude. So the setup is behaving the way the physics says it should.

What I would change next

The simulation points to clear improvements for the next version.

  • Taper the rear block instead of ending it flat, to shrink the wake and cut drag.
  • Smooth the step where the body rises into the rear block, so the air stays attached.
  • Run the same study on each new version and compare the drag force side by side.
