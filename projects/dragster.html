Wooden Dragster

Designed in SolidWorks, tested in a virtual wind tunnel

I modeled a wooden dragster, then used SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation to see how air moves around it and where it creates drag.

The simulation setup

An external flow study puts the car inside a virtual wind tunnel. The air comes straight at the nose, like the car is racing forward.

Drag force at 20 m/s 0.11 N

About the weight of 11 grams. The study converged: the drag value settled within the solver's criteria.

Why 20 m/s

That is a typical top speed for a small CO2 dragster, so the results reflect what the car actually sees on the track.

What the air does

The flow lines tell the story in three parts.

20 m/s

At the nose

Air arrives at full speed and splits around the pointed nose.

22 m/s

Over the body

Air speeds up as it squeezes over the shoulders and the raised rear block.

~0 m/s

Behind the rear

The flat back cuts the air off. It stalls and swirls, which pulls the car backward.

Does it make sense?

A quick hand check

When moving air hits a surface head-on, its pressure rises by about

½ ρ v² = ½ × 1.2 kg/m³ × (20 m/s)² ≈ 240 Pa

The simulation shows a pressure spread of about 290 Pa around the car, the same order of magnitude. So the setup is behaving the way the physics says it should.

What I would change next

The simulation points to clear improvements for the next version.

  • Taper the rear block instead of ending it flat, to shrink the wake and cut drag.
  • Smooth the step where the body rises into the rear block, so the air stays attached.
  • Run the same study on each new version and compare the drag force side by side.
